## Project Overview
This project looks at whether Marvel and DC comics reflected, led or lagged
changes in American women's work since the 1940s. We compare the occupations
of comic book characters decade by decade with U.S. Census occupation data
and women's labor force participation rates.

## Data Sources
- **Marvel Database:** 37,204 characters
- **DC Database:** 14,671 characters
- **IPUMS USA:** U.S. occupations by sex, 1940 to 2023
- **FRED:** women's labor force participation, 1948 to 2026

## Key Findings
1. **Comics best match the workforce of about 7 years earlier.** The female
   share of new characters moves with women's labor force participation
   (r = 0.83), and the match is strongest against the workforce of 7 years
   earlier (r = 0.89).
2. **The roster is still about 70% male, but new characters changed fast.**
   The female share of new characters rose from 11.5% in 1960 to 40.0% in 2025.
3. **Female characters get "women's jobs."** Their job groups are on average
   50.1% female in the real workforce, compared with 42.9% for male characters.
   In historically male jobs, female characters reached 20% only in the 2010s,
   while real women did in the 1980s.
4. **Women are written with fewer working lives.** 57.1% of female characters
   have an occupation listed, compared with 66.7% of male characters.
5. **Marvel and DC follow society at about the same pace.** DC's best match
   is a 5 year lag and Marvel's is 7 years, but the difference is too small
   to say either publisher mirrors reality better.
